# Account Entry Strategy

Use this reference after customer intelligence, fit scoring, and value assessment. It turns research into a practical plan for entering one account.

The goal is not to list products. The goal is to show how the user's offer expands the customer's business, solves a sourcing gap, or gives the customer a better option for a specific project.

## Round 1 Triage

Use round 1 when the input is minimal: company name, contact name, LinkedIn profile, website, or a raw lead file.

Independently verify three questions before building a full account plan:

1. Product fit: does the customer actually sell, buy, specify, or influence products adjacent to the user's offer?
2. Decision authority: is the named contact likely able to choose suppliers, introduce products, set pricing, or influence projects?
3. Structural blockers: local-made-only positioning, artisan-only model, very small scale, fixed supplier system, no relevant category, or long corporate procurement chain.

Star rating:

| Rating | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Strong product fit, clear decision-maker, no hard blocker | Build full account file and high-personalization outreach |
| 4 | Good fit with one blocker such as scale, title, or origin concern | Build account file and use active outreach |
| 3 | Partial fit, uncertain authority, or small ceiling | Keep in CRM; light-touch only |
| 2 | Category overlap but weak reachability or long decision chain | Archive/nurture |
| 1 | Local-only hard blocker or product mismatch | Reject |

Do not let an optimistic original note override independent evidence. If the first named contact is not a decision-maker, search for the real buyer and downgrade the lead one level if no better contact is found.

## Decision-Maker Route

Prefer contacts by company size and role:

| Context | Priority contact | Entry angle |
|---|---|---|
| Small company | Owner, founder, principal, president | Supplier cooperation, margin, new category |
| Mid-sized distributor/importer | Procurement, sourcing, buyer, category manager, head of buying | Reliable supply, category expansion, project sourcing |
| Dealer/decorator | Owner, sales/account manager, production manager | Help win customer projects; complement their decoration workflow |
| End buyer | Brand manager, people experience, HR, marketing, event manager | Onboarding, recognition, campaign, VIP gifting |
| Large company | Category, procurement, program owner | Confirm decision chain before pitching |

Avoid treating general inquiries, customer service, and junior marketing contacts as buyers unless they are the only available bridge.

## Buying Trigger Analysis

Look for a reason to contact now:

- Recent social posts showing events, awards, new clients, product launches, seasonal campaigns, new store/branch, or trade show activity.
- Website updates: new catalog, new quote form, new category page, case study, portfolio, blog, or hiring page.
- Business-cycle triggers: year-end gifting, onboarding kits, realtor closing gifts, recognition programs, sponsor gifts, sports tournaments, conference swag, VIP/client gifts.
- Capability triggers: customer has engraving, UV printing, kitting, company stores, fulfillment, or gift-box curation that can use better blanks or custom metal pieces.
- Pain signals: reviews or posts mentioning quality, delay, customization, price, low perceived value, sourcing, or product gaps.

### Hook Discovery — Three-Step Process

Before writing outreach, find one or two specific hooks through these steps in order:

**Step 1 — Contact's recent activity (strongest hook):**
- Fetch the contact's LinkedIn personal page.
- Look for posts within the last 30 days.
- Found → use `Saw your post on...` as opening; most persuasive because it is personal and timely.
- Not found → move to step 2.

**Step 2 — Company recent activity:**
- Search `"{company name}" site:linkedin.com` for recent company page posts.
- Search `"{company name}" 2025 OR 2026 news award expansion`.
- Awards / new products / expansion / hiring → record and use as invite hook.
- Not found → move to step 3.

**Step 3 — Public client work or served industries:**
- Fetch company Portfolio / Case Studies / Clients page.
- Identify the primary industries served.
- Map against relevant use cases (tech / finance / mining / healthcare / sports).
- Use `Saw you work a lot with {industry} clients...` as fallback hook.

Output: one or two specific hooks written into the account file before drafting outreach. Do not open with generic praise when a concrete hook is available.

Classify the trigger:

| Trigger strength | Definition | Outreach use |
|---|---|---|
| Strong | Recent public activity directly related to use case | Lead the first message with it |
| Medium | Stable business model suggests recurring need | Use as account angle, not as "urgent" claim |
| Weak | Only generic category fit | Keep message low pressure |
| None | No current reason found | Nurture or research later |

## Supplier-Switching Hypothesis

Do not assume the goal is replacing an existing supplier. Choose one hypothesis:

- Replacement supplier: customer likely has supplier pain, weak category coverage, poor material quality, or price/margin pressure.
- Backup supplier: customer already has suppliers; offer a second option for specific projects, short runs, or new materials.
- Category expansion: customer sells adjacent promo/gift products but lacks premium/custom metal items.
- Blank/component partner: customer has local decoration capability and may need better metal blanks.
- Custom project partner: customer works on bespoke/OEM/campaign projects and may need sourcing support.
- Market intelligence lead: not an immediate buyer but useful for learning product expectations.

Switching blockers to record:

- Local-made or domestic-only promise.
- Fast-turnaround business model that cannot wait for overseas production.
- Very low-volume one-off retail orders.
- Existing exclusive supplier or locked catalog.
- Strong apparel-only / print-only focus with no premium gift use case.
- Extreme price sensitivity.

## Entry Angles

Create three ranked entry angles for qualified accounts. Each angle must include:

- Why it is valid, based on customer evidence.
- One English hook sentence suitable for email or LinkedIn.
- Product/scenario pairing.
- Risk or first-order trap.

Common angle patterns:

| Customer type | Strong angle | Secondary angle | Long-term angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| Promotional distributor | Fill premium metal category gaps | Cross-sell with existing corporate gifts | Exclusive or private-label metal series |
| Dealer/decorator | Upgrade perceived value with metal blanks | Support last-mile engraving/printing | Backup supplier for special projects |
| Gift box / hamper company | Add lasting premium metal pieces inside boxes | Reduce scattered metal-item sourcing | Co-branded gift set series |
| Awards / recognition company | Custom metal recognition pieces | Sponsor/VIP/event gifts | Repeat annual recognition programs |
| Realtor / real estate group | Closing gifts and client keepsakes | Team awards and referral gifts | Seasonal referral program |
| End buyer | Onboarding/year-end/VIP kits | Event or campaign gifts | Annual corporate gift program |

## Scenario x Product Pairing

Before outreach, list 4-6 customer scenarios and map each to 2-4 products or product families. Do not dump the full catalog.

For Wischos/custom metal gift work, remember that the catalog is a reference, not the boundary. Use existing pages for evidence, but include custom metal gift sets and adjacent custom metal gifts when the customer's use case supports them.

Product examples to use when relevant:

- Brass pens, titanium EDC, stainless desk pieces.
- Metal bookmarks, card cases, key organizers, bottle openers.
- Tumblers, metal keepsakes, custom-shaped metal pieces.
- Gift sets combining pen, desk, EDC, drinkware, packaging, logo, finish, and material customization.

## Outreach Material Plan

For high-priority accounts, prepare:

- LinkedIn invite under 300 characters.
- First email with one researched hook and no more than three product directions.
- One short follow-up angle.
- One objection response for "we already have suppliers."
- One MOQ response such as a controlled pilot, when true.
- Sample-pack concept only when the account has enough value to justify it.

Do not send a full catalog, price list, sample demand, or broad company introduction in the first message.

## Multi-Touch Cadence

Use a measured cadence unless the user specifies otherwise:

| Day | Channel | Action |
|---:|---|---|
| 0 | LinkedIn | Send personalized invite |
| 3-5 | Social / website | Light interaction or contact-form fallback if appropriate |
| 7 | Email | Send research-based first email after contact path is confirmed |
| 14 | Email/LinkedIn | Follow up with one concrete product/scenario idea or mockup concept |
| 30 | CRM | Pause if no response |
| 60 | Email/LinkedIn | Re-enter with a different angle if the account value justifies it |
| 90 | CRM | Recheck for new trigger before another attempt |

Always respect anti-spam rules, opt-out requests, and platform norms.
