# Argos-Type Customer Persona

**Reference document for distributor-screening Skill**
**Version:** v1.0 — 2026-06-05
**Source:** Reverse-engineered from the Argos Promotional Products (NZ) interaction pattern

---

## Purpose

This persona codifies the "ideal Wischos Gift distributor customer" based on the observed behavior of Argos Promotional Products (NZ) — the prospect with whom Wischos had its highest-quality engagement to date, even without converting to an immediate sale.

The Argos relationship demonstrated that **the goal is not just closing one quote, but identifying customers whose engagement pattern signals long-term partnership potential**. This persona is the template for finding more of them.

Use this document as a reference companion to `SKILL.md` when:
- Evaluating a new prospect against the "Argos-likeness" standard
- Designing first-touch messaging
- Filtering LinkedIn / Google search results
- Deciding which prospects to prioritize from a large pool

---

## One-line definition

> A **New Zealand or Australia-based**, owner-operated promotional products distributor with **5-30 employees**, **direct importer status**, **mixed mid-to-premium product mix** including occasional metal/curated programs, where the **Founder or Director personally responds** to outreach within 3-7 days.

## Hard demographic profile

| Dimension | Argos-type characteristic |
|---|---|
| **Primary geography** | NZ and AU |
| **Secondary geography** | SG, UAE (same engagement pattern, different culture overlay) |
| **Headcount** | 5-30 employees (Argos ~10-15) |
| **Years in business** | 5+ years — mature operation, established supplier network |
| **Decision-maker access** | Founder / Director / MD answers email personally |
| **Decision cycle** | 3-7 days to first substantive reply, no RFP process |
| **Import behavior** | Direct importer (has importer code, books LCL containers) |
| **Product mix** | Drinkware + metal pens + premium notebooks + occasional gift sets — not single-category |
| **Working price band** | NZ\$20-80 per unit per item (mid-tier) |
| **Client base** | Corporate B2B — not retail / consumer |
| **Language signature** | "considered" / "thoughtful" / "curated" / "bespoke" / "client-led" |

## Behavioral profile (the irreplaceable part)

These behavioral signals are what make an Argos-type customer **high value** even when they don't immediately buy. They cannot be inferred from a website alone — they appear in the first 1-2 email exchanges.

| Behavior observed in Argos | Signal interpretation |
|---|---|
| **Founder Satish replies personally** (not via assistant or sales rep) | Direct decision authority, no internal layers |
| **Willingly shares competitor pricing** (NZ\$28.68 / NZ\$33.99 references) | Treats supplier as collaborator, not adversary |
| **Says "a little high"** (NZ understatement) rather than ghosting | Commercial maturity — doesn't waste supplier time |
| **Specifies that price isn't the only factor** | Not pure commodity-driven |
| **Leaves door open on rejection** ("new sets as/when available") | Values long-term supplier relationships |
| **Sends concrete enquiry** (SKU + quantity + destination) | Real buying intent, not fishing |
| **Distinguishes metal vs non-metal product tiers** | Has product literacy |
| **Acknowledges supplier's effort** in reply tone | Treats trade partnership as professional, not transactional |

**Threshold:** A prospect exhibiting **5 or more of these 8 behaviors** in the first 2 exchanges = high-confidence Argos-type → prioritize for nurture.

---

## Anti-Argos signals (instant disqualification)

These signals indicate the prospect is NOT Argos-type, regardless of how their company looks on paper:

| Anti-signal | Why it disqualifies |
|---|---|
| Reply comes from sales rep / account exec, not Founder/Director | Decision-maker is layered behind staff — slow cycles |
| Uses RFP / tender / formal procurement process | Won't engage with SOHO supplier — needs scale |
| Replies are pure yes/no with no explanation | No relationship-building intent |
| Ghosts after initial enquiry (no reply within 3 weeks) | Lacks commercial maturity |
| Opens with "send us your best price" | Pure price-driven, commodity tier |
| Auto-RFQ signature with "no follow-up please" | Programmatic procurement |
| LinkedIn company page hides Founder/Director identity | Decision-makers not accessible |
| Communicates in dense, transactional, no-pleasantries tone | Wrong culture fit for Wischos's understated voice |

## "Argos-Likeness" Score (0-10)

Award 1 point for each TRUE statement (verifiable via LinkedIn + website in under 5 minutes):

```
□ Owner / Director / MD visible on LinkedIn and posts personally
□ 5-30 employees on LinkedIn company page
□ Imports directly (stated in About or implied via supplier mentions)
□ Website features case studies or named client logos
□ Uses "bespoke" / "curated" / "considered" / "tailored" language
□ Posted within last 30 days (LinkedIn or website news)
□ Mixed product mix — drinkware, metal items, gift sets (not single category)
□ Operates in single country (not multinational mega-distributor)
□ Client base includes B2B corporates, not consumer retail
□ No Tier 0 hard-kill keywords on homepage (10K+ SKU, factory direct, etc.)

───────────────────────────────────────
Score ≥ 7 = High-confidence Argos-type → prioritize approach
Score 5-6 = Borderline → park, watch for 60 days
Score < 5 = Not Argos-type → process through standard SKILL.md flow
```

**Note:** This score is **additive on top of** the standard SKILL.md Tier 3 composite score — it's a secondary filter for prioritization, not a replacement for the screening funnel. A prospect must first survive Tier 0-2 before being scored for Argos-likeness.

## First-touch playbook for Argos-type prospects

Based on what worked in the Argos exchange, the optimal first contact follows this 4-line structure (total ~80 words):

```
1. Market acknowledgment (1 line)
   → "Saw [company name] serves [their specific niche] in [market]."

2. Specific spec signal (1 line — the key differentiator)
   → "We work mainly on metal sets — brass crown, anodised aluminium body,
       stainless steel accents — for executive and milestone briefs."

3. Soft offer with no push (1 line)
   → "Capability sheet attached if useful for future briefs."

4. Nurture door (1 line)
   → "No urgency — happy to be on file when a metal-leaning enquiry
       comes up at your end."

Sign off: "Best regards, John"
```

This is the **Scenario C — Cold first outreach** template from the `wischos-email-voice` Skill, calibrated specifically for Argos-type recipients.

**Why this works for Argos-type:**
- No pricing claims (they'll price-shop locally first anyway)
- Specific material vocabulary signals trade competence
- "Future briefs" framing matches their long-cycle thinking
- No urgency respects their owner-operator pace
- ~80 words respects their inbox

## Confirmed Argos-type customer record

For reference, the canonical Argos-type customer interaction looked like this:

| Touchpoint | What happened |
|---|---|
| **Initial enquiry** | Satish enquired about 500x vacuum set (specific SKU + quantity + destination NZ) |
| **First response time** | Within 24 hours of John's reply |
| **Tone** | Professional, concise, no fluff |
| **Decision feedback** | "A little high" — included two local reference prices |
| **Door state on close** | Open: "Happy to receive information about new sets as/when they become available" |
| **Net relationship status** | Permission-to-nurture granted (no sale, but qualified for future) |

This sequence — **enquiry → quick reply → honest feedback → open door** — is the gold-standard Argos-type pattern. Future prospects exhibiting it should be treated as **highest-priority nurture targets**, even without immediate revenue.
