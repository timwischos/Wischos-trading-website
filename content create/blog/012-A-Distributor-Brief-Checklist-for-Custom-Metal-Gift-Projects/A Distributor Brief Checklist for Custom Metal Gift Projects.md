## Why the Brief Matters

For promo distributors, a custom metal gift project usually starts with an imperfect client sentence:

> "We need something premium for top customers."  
> "Can you find something different this year?"  
> "The client wants a metal gift set, but budget is still being discussed."

Those are useful buying signals, but they are not enough for a supplier quote.

Standard catalogue workflows work well when the distributor needs speed, proven SKUs, and repeatable everyday items. ASI describes ESP+ as a centralized platform with access to more than 1.2 million brandable products, while SAGE positions its tools around product search, presentations, CRM, and business management for promotional product companies. Those systems are built for comparison and speed.

Custom metal projects need a different starting point. Before a supplier can quote responsibly, they need to understand the commercial use case, technical requirements, approval path, and risk tolerance behind the project.

This checklist is written for distributors, not end buyers. The goal is to help you collect enough information from your client before you ask a custom metal supplier for pricing.

## 1. Project Purpose

Start with the business reason for the gift.

Ask your client:

- Is this for client appreciation, employee recognition, onboarding, a conference, a sponsor package, a milestone, or a year-end program?
- Is the item meant to be kept, carried, displayed, or handed out quickly?
- Is the main goal perceived value, brand visibility, retention, recognition, or event utility?

This matters because the same product category can behave differently across projects. A metal pen can be a low-cost giveaway, a service award component, a boardroom desk gift, or part of a premium set. The product name alone does not tell the supplier which direction to price or design around.

**Brief field to collect:**  
Project purpose and what the client needs the gift to achieve.

## 2. Recipient Profile

Distributors often receive a product request before they receive the recipient profile. That creates weak quotes.

Ask:

- Who receives the gift?
- Are they executives, field teams, employees, top clients, event guests, sponsors, members, dealers, or sales partners?
- Will the gift be used in an office, on site, in travel, at home, or at an event?
- Does the recipient group need one universal item, or should the project split into tiers?

PPAI's Product Power 2026 research points to a shift toward personal relevance, design appeal, functionality and emotional connection in branded merchandise. That is a useful signal for distributors: a better brief should describe the recipient context, not only the product category.

**Brief field to collect:**  
Recipient type, environment and whether the gift is broad-audience or selected-recipient.

## 3. Quantity and Repeat Potential

Quantity affects far more than unit price.

It affects:

- whether existing product families can be adapted
- whether custom tooling is realistic
- whether packaging can be customized
- whether samples should be made from stock blanks or project-specific materials
- whether the supplier should think about repeat production

For a planned custom metal gift project, an approximate quantity range is more useful than no number at all. If your client says "not sure yet," ask for a working scenario: 100, 250, 500, 1,000, or phased orders.

Also ask whether the project may repeat annually or across multiple client locations. A one-time 100-set project and a 100-set pilot for a recurring recognition program should be quoted differently.

**Brief field to collect:**  
Expected quantity, possible reorder, and whether this is a one-time project or pilot.

## 4. Target Budget

Many weak custom quotes happen because the client avoids budget.

For distributors, budget is not just a buying number. It determines whether the project should be:

- catalogue-adjacent
- semi-custom
- fully bespoke
- a single item
- a two-piece or three-piece set
- standard boxed
- upgraded with custom insert, sleeve, liner, or branded packaging

Ask for a target budget per piece or per set. If the client has distributor margin expectations, that also needs to be clear before supplier quoting. A supplier quote that ignores the distributor's resale margin is not commercially useful.

Do not ask only for "best price." Ask for the price lane the project must fit.

**Brief field to collect:**  
Target sell price or landed budget per item/set, plus whether distributor margin is already included.

## 5. Deadline and In-Hands Date

For custom metal gifts, the date that matters is not the date the client wants to approve the order. It is the in-hands date.

Ask:

- What is the required delivery date?
- Is there an event date?
- Is the date fixed or flexible?
- Does the client need samples before approval?
- Does the shipment go to one location or multiple addresses?

The distinction between production lead time and project lead time is critical. Production can only start after product direction, artwork, sample or proof approval, material availability, packaging details and deposit are aligned. Wischos typically discusses production around 25-35 days after approval, but the full project timeline depends on sampling, packaging, inspection, export and shipping.

For Q4 and year-end projects, distributors should ask this early. If the client wants something more specific than a standard giveaway, the project needs time for proofing and production decisions.

**Brief field to collect:**  
Event date, required in-hands date, sample need, approval deadline and delivery address type.

## 6. Product Format

"Metal gift" is too broad for quoting.

Ask whether the client wants:

- one item
- paired items
- a three-piece set
- a four-piece executive set
- a recognition piece
- a custom-shaped item
- a kit built around an occasion

Then ask whether the product format is fixed or open to supplier recommendation.

This is where distributor language matters. If your client says "we want a metal keychain," the supplier can quote a product. If your client says "we need a practical recognition item for 250 dealer partners under a fixed deadline," the supplier can recommend a better product lane.

**Brief field to collect:**  
Single item, set, custom-shaped piece, recognition item or open recommendation.

## 7. Material and Finish Direction

Metal choice is not only aesthetic. It affects weight, cost, logo result, perceived value and production route.

Useful material directions include:

- stainless steel for durability and daily handling
- aluminium for lighter weight, machining and anodised color options
- brass for warmer weight and executive desk presence
- titanium for lightweight premium drinkware or EDC positioning
- zinc alloy for shaped pieces, badges, medals, tags and cost-controlled molded forms

Finish direction matters too:

- brushed finish for daily handling and scratch tolerance
- matte or sandblasted finish for low-reflection modern surfaces
- anodising for aluminium color
- PVD for premium dark finishes on suitable metals
- plating for badges, medals, pins and shaped pieces
- enamel or color fill for pins, medals and identity pieces

Do not force the client to choose the exact process too early. But do collect the intended look, handling environment and brand color expectations. That gives the supplier enough technical context to advise.

**Brief field to collect:**  
Preferred material, finish, color direction, handling environment and whether the client is open to alternatives.

## 8. Logo and Artwork Requirements

Custom metal gifts can fail at the logo stage even when the product is right.

Ask:

- Does the client need exact brand color reproduction?
- Is a monochrome engraved mark acceptable?
- Will the logo be subtle, visible, tone-on-tone, or packaging-led?
- Are individual names or variable data required?
- Is vector artwork available?
- Are there restricted brand clear-space rules?

Technical note: laser engraving is usually strong for metal because it is durable and does not add an ink layer on top of the surface. But it is not the right answer for every artwork. Exact Pantone color matching, gradients or full-color logos may require printing, enamel, color fill, labels or packaging-based branding instead.

The supplier should see artwork before confirming the final logo method. A simple wordmark, a detailed crest, a multi-color logo and tiny text all behave differently on metal.

**Brief field to collect:**  
Logo file, color requirements, size expectation, visibility level, personalization needs and artwork restrictions.

## 9. Packaging and Presentation

For distributor-led projects, packaging is often where perceived value is won or lost.

Ask:

- Does the gift need standard packaging or retail-style presentation?
- Is the box part of the brand experience?
- Should the product sit in EVA, foam, paperboard insert, tray, pouch or sleeve?
- Does the client need a message card, QR code, insert card or campaign note?
- Is the package going into a larger event kit or shipping directly as a gift?

Packaging affects cost, production time, shipping volume and damage risk. It also affects how easily you can present the project to your client. A metal pen in a polybag and a metal pen in a rigid gift box with a fitted insert are not the same sales offer.

**Brief field to collect:**  
Box type, insert preference, branding on packaging, insert card needs, shipping use and whether packaging is part of the proposal value.

## 10. Destination, Import and Documentation

A distributor brief should include destination details before final quote.

Ask:

- Destination country
- Delivery address type
- Preferred Incoterm, if known
- Whether the client needs landed cost, FOB, CIF, DDP or local handoff pricing
- Any labeling, material declaration or documentation expectations
- Whether the goods enter as a single shipment or split shipment

This is not only a logistics detail. It affects packaging cartons, documentation, shipping method, duty planning and the quote structure. For international sourcing, the supplier cannot responsibly discuss lead time or cost without knowing where the goods are going.

PPAI's 2024 U.S. distributor sales reporting notes that distributors are diversifying sourcing strategies, with $4.3 billion in sales coming from outside traditional networks. ASI has also reported that more than six in ten distributors sourced direct in 2024. Wider sourcing is now normal, but it increases the need for clearer import and documentation details.

**Brief field to collect:**  
Destination, shipment structure, Incoterm preference, documentation requirements and whether a landed-cost estimate is needed.

## 11. Approval Path

Custom projects need a clear approval path before quoting becomes production.

Ask:

- Who approves the product direction?
- Who approves artwork?
- Who approves sample or pre-production proof?
- Who approves packaging?
- Does procurement need a spec sheet?
- Does legal or brand need to review the logo method?
- How long does client approval usually take?

Many project delays are not factory delays. They are approval delays. A distributor can protect the relationship by identifying the approval chain early.

**Brief field to collect:**  
Decision makers, approval stages, required documents and expected approval timing.

## 12. Risk Questions Before You Quote

Before sending the supplier request, ask yourself:

| Risk Area | Distributor Question |
|---|---|
| Timeline | Is there enough time for proofing, production, inspection and shipping? |
| Artwork | Has the client supplied usable logo files and realistic logo expectations? |
| Material | Does the requested material fit the budget and use case? |
| Packaging | Is packaging part of the perceived value or only transit protection? |
| Quantity | Is the quantity realistic for the level of customization requested? |
| Compliance | Does the client need material declarations, testing, labeling or restricted-use clarification? |
| Cost | Does the client need FOB, landed cost or distributor resale pricing? |
| Approval | Who can actually approve the sample and final artwork? |

If several answers are unknown, the project is not ready for a firm custom quote. It is ready for a feasibility review.

## FAQ

### What should a distributor include in a custom metal gift brief?

Include project purpose, recipient profile, quantity, target budget, deadline, destination, product format, material or finish direction, logo requirements, packaging expectations, sample needs, documentation requirements and approval path.

### Should distributors ask for budget before requesting a supplier quote?

Yes. Budget determines whether the project should stay catalogue-adjacent, move into semi-custom work, or be reviewed as a bespoke project. Without a budget lane, suppliers may quote technically valid options that do not fit the distributor's resale position.

### What artwork does a custom metal supplier need?

Vector artwork is preferred for logos, especially for engraving, debossing, enamel, plating or packaging artwork. The supplier should also know whether exact brand colors, subtle logo placement, individual names or full-color artwork are required.

### When is a custom metal project not ready for a quote?

It is not ready for a firm quote when quantity, deadline, budget, destination, product format or approval path are still unknown. In that case, the right next step is a feasibility review, not final pricing.

### Can a supplier recommend products if the distributor does not know the exact item?

Yes, if the brief includes purpose, recipient, quantity, budget, deadline and destination. A strong supplier can recommend a product lane from those constraints even when the client has not chosen the exact item.
